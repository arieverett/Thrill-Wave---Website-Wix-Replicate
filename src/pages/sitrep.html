SITREP is our research. Before we film anything, we dig deep into your craft, your peers, your industry and the wider field you work in. Think of it as your own research department, one that sits outside your walls, cares a lot about the story and lines up with what you're trying to get done. People come to us for our expertise, and this is how we put it to work: we do the homework, form a point of view and help steer the story. Then we go film it.

Why we bother

Most films that miss were decided before anyone picked up a camera. Sometimes the idea came from a gut feeling that nobody checked against the people it was meant for. Sometimes a peer made something similar, so it seemed like the thing to do, even though nobody asked whether it fit. Sometimes a vendor showed up with an answer before anyone had named the question.

And sometimes the film was good, but nobody planned who needed to see it or where, so it ended up sitting in a folder. A little research up front heads off all of that, and it usually turns up a better story than anyone expected.

Three films, one way of working

We made three separate films for ITCA, the Inter Tribal Council of Arizona. In every one, the research changed what we made, and by the time we picked up a camera we knew exactly what the film had to do. Here's what each SITREP found and what we made because of it:

WIC Nutrition Services

  1. 1Situation

    ITCA wanted more families to enroll in WIC. Plenty of families qualified, but participation kept dropping.

  2. 2What we found

    Many tribal members connected WIC with government surveillance. The problem was trust, not awareness.

  3. 3What we made

    Tribal mothers speak straight to camera to other mothers. There's no narrator and no official voice telling anyone what to do.

Tribal Epidemiology Center

  1. 1Situation

    The Center needed vaccine education for communities that have good reasons to doubt government health programs.

  2. 2What we found

    The usual health messaging made things worse. People wanted to hear from their own leaders, not from outside authorities.

  3. 3What we made

    Tribal health directors and community members explain it in their own words. We shot it on location in their communities.

Civic Engagement: Voter Education

  1. 1Situation

    ITCA asked for a ten-part docu-series to help more tribal members vote in the 2026 election.

  2. 2What we found

    People weren't staying home out of apathy. Staying home was a reasonable response to a long history of broken promises, and the series had to face that history first.

  3. 3What we made

    "Your Voice, Your Power" is ten episodes with tribal leaders, elders and young voters. It's in production now.
